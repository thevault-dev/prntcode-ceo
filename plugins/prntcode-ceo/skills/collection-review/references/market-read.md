# The market read

The second half of the collection review. The range plan says what customers asked for; the market read says where the season is going and where a print-first brand like PRNTCODE can win. Together they're the starting point for Hessa and the designer.

## What it may and may not do

- **It never changes the shape.** Counts per family and the named silhouettes come from sales data only. The market read can say "the season backs this" or "the season is moving away from this", but the numbers stay as the range plan gave them.
- **It can suggest directions for the open slots,** labelled "market says", as options for the designer, never as decisions. One to three directions per open family, each tied to its sources.
- **It doesn't choose PRNTCODE's prints, quantities or designs.** It reports what the market is doing with print (which families of print, how print-led brands use them). Which PRNTCODE print goes where stays Hessa's call. Where the market raises a print question (for example, whether to keep a house print running season after season), it's put as a question for Hessa.

## Sources

Fresh research for each run, from fashion trade press and serious editorial, never SEO trend blogs, marketplaces' "trend" pages or Google reviews.

| Strand | Go to first | Also good |
|---|---|---|
| Runway direction | Vogue Runway, Business of Fashion, WWD, Vogue Business | Wallpaper*, Monocle, Dazed, i-D, The Cut, W, Numéro, ELLE, Harper's Bazaar, FashionUnited |
| Forecasting, colour and fabric | WGSN and Coloro (public releases), Pantone, Heuritech, Première Vision | Edited, Lyst Index |
| Business of print-led brands | BoF (case studies), Vogue Business, Glossy, FT, Drapers, company results | Ragtrader, Modaes, FashionUnited, Fashion Dive, Puck |
| The Gulf | Vogue Arabia, Harper's Bazaar Arabia, Grazia Middle East, The National, Gulf News, Khaleej Times, Arabian Business | AGBI, Chalhoub Group reports, DinarStandard / Salaam Gateway (State of the Global Islamic Economy), Ounass and Chalhoub press |

Rules for sources:
- Every claim carries its source and date, as [Title](URL).
- A trend is **widespread** only with two or more independent sources; otherwise it's marked single-source.
- Say what couldn't be reached (paywalls, blocked sites) rather than filling the gap from memory.
- Prices on retailer pages are marked "seen <date>".

## Which season to read, per line

| Line | Read | When the coverage lands |
|---|---|---|
| `rtw-ss` | the Spring/Summer runway shows for the launch year (SS27 for an April 2027 launch), plus Resort and Pre-Spring when out | Sept–Oct of the year before |
| `rtw-fw` | the Fall/Winter shows for the launch year | Feb–March |
| abaya drops | Ramadan and Eid capsules (international and regional), Gulf modest-fashion coverage, the matching runway season for colour and fabric | Ramadan capsules from about 6 weeks before Ramadan |
| `jalabiya` | last year's Ramadan and Eid capsules, regional kaftan and jalabiya labels | as above |
| `swimwear` | Resort and SS swim coverage, resort-wear retail | autumn before |

If the season's shows haven't happened yet, use the latest forecasts and the season before, and say so at the top.

## What to cover

1. **The season in one paragraph.** Is print up or down this season, and where (city by city if it splits)?
2. **Print and pattern.** The print families that led (florals and what kind, animal, stripes, geometric, conversational or novelty, woven pattern), with designers named. What's thin or absent.
3. **Silhouettes, family by family** (tops, bottoms, dresses, or the line's own families). What's rising, and what the sources say is fading. Mark anything only one source saw.
4. **Colour and fabric.** Forecast colours, then what the runways actually showed. Fabrics, judged against the climate the line sells into (for the Gulf summer: breathable natural fibres, light colours, cover from the sun).
5. **The Gulf lens.** What covers up well; which trends need a lined, layered or longer version for PRNTCODE's customer; how regional editors read the season.
6. **The line's calendar.** The occasions and sale periods inside its selling window (Eids, summer travel and where people go, National Day, Dubai Summer Surprises and the Dubai Shopping Festival), and what each means for when to push full price.
7. **Where print-first brands thrive.** Two to five cases from trade press (what they sell, price, channels, how they use signature prints, what's going well or badly), then the lessons for a small print-first brand.
8. **Where PRNTCODE can win.** The whitespace: what the regional competitors and e-tailers don't offer, where PRNTCODE's price sits against the brands it's compared with, the channels that suit it.
9. **How the shape lines up with the season.** For each family in the range plan: whether the season backs the named silhouettes, and one to three "market says" directions for the open slots.
10. **Questions for Hessa and Khaled.** At most five, each something the market raises that the data can't answer.

## Doing the research

When subagents are available, run three strands in parallel (runway direction; print-led brands; the Gulf market), each told the sources above, the line, its launch and selling window, and PRNTCODE's context (print-first, own prints, AED price range, Khaleeji customers with expats as the next audience, made in-house in small runs, sells best in person and on WhatsApp). Then write the read yourself from their reports, checking the claims the conclusions lean on against the source before using them.

A read is good for about four weeks, or until the next show season. Save it next to the sales data as `market-read-<line>-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md` and reuse it within that window instead of researching again.

## Delivering it

- **In chat:** after the range plan, a market read of no more than 8 lines: the season in a sentence, the "market says" directions for each open family, and the single biggest opportunity.
- **In depth:** the full read as a document (the Docs type when the session lists it; otherwise a markdown file sent with `SendUserFile`), with a Sources list at the end.
